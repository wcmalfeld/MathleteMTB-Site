College & Beyond Mentorship

A clearer path from high school to what’s next.

College, career, and life after high school can feel like a lot to figure out at once. Mathlete MTB helps students explore their options, build a practical plan, and take the next steps with confidence — from applications and affordability research to the habits that support a successful transition into adulthood.

Request a free introductory conversation Explore what’s included ↓

Who This Is For

This service is for high school students who want help making sense of what comes next. A student might have a clear college goal, several competing interests, no idea what they want to do, or simply need help breaking a big process into manageable steps. The work is personalized and collaborative, with parents or guardians included in agreed-upon planning conversations.

  • Students exploring college, trade, gap-year, or other post-high-school options
  • Students who need help organizing deadlines and following through
  • Student-athletes and outdoor-oriented learners who want their interests and strengths considered as part of their future
  • Families seeking a clearer understanding of cost, aid offers, and realistic options

What We’ll Work Toward

  1. A clearer picture of the student’s interests, strengths, priorities, and possible next steps.
  2. A research-based college or post-high-school option shortlist.
  3. A manageable timeline with milestones, tasks, and deadlines.
  4. Better-organized application materials and feedback on student-written essays.
  5. A structured way to compare estimated costs, aid offers, and scholarship opportunities.
  6. Practical routines for time management, self-advocacy, study habits, and the transition to greater independence.

What’s Included

Four Areas of Support

Every plan is built around the student. Most draw on more than one of these areas, in whatever mix fits.

01Direction

Explore direction

Map interests, strengths, priorities, and possible pathways. Research colleges, programs, trades, gap-year possibilities, and career directions relevant to the student.

02Applications

Plan applications

Build a timeline, organize requirements, develop an activities résumé, brainstorm and review student-written essays, and prepare for relevant interviews. SAT/ACT planning or math tutoring may be included when appropriate.

03Affordability

Understand affordability

Help the family compare published costs and financial-aid offers, organize scholarship research, track deadlines, and prepare useful questions for financial-aid offices.

04Independence

Build independence

Develop realistic study routines, time management, self-advocacy, goal setting, and a first-semester plan so the student can practice managing responsibilities with less day-to-day parental prompting.

How It Works

Four Steps

  1. 01

    Start with a conversation

    Learn what the student and family are trying to solve and decide whether the service is a fit.

  2. 02

    Build a personalized plan

    Agree on priorities, scope, timeline, and how the student and parent/guardian will participate.

  3. 03

    Meet, work, and follow through

    Use one-to-one sessions, action items, and agreed check-ins to make progress.

  4. 04

    Review the next step

    Adjust the plan as interests, applications, offers, or circumstances change.

Questions

Does my student need to know what they want to do?

No. Exploring options and narrowing the next step can be part of the work.

Is this only for students applying to four-year colleges?

No. The goal is to help students make an informed plan. Depending on the student, research may include college, certificate or trade programs, a gap year, or other pathways.

Can you help with college essays?

Yes — through brainstorming, feedback, structure, and revision coaching. Essays remain the student’s own work; the service does not write or submit essays for them.

Can you guarantee admission or scholarships?

No. Admissions decisions and scholarship awards are made by the relevant institutions or organizations. The service helps students prepare, research, and stay organized.

Can you help us understand the cost of college?

The service can help organize research on published costs, scholarship opportunities, and financial-aid offers, and prepare questions for college financial-aid offices. It does not replace a financial-aid professional, tax professional, or financial advisor.

Do you also offer math or test-prep support?

Math tutoring and relevant test-prep planning can be discussed as part of an individual plan, based on the student’s needs and availability. More about tutoring.

How involved are parents?

Parents/guardians can be included in planning conversations and agreed-upon progress updates. The student should also have a meaningful voice in the plan.

Is the service virtual or in person?

Both. Sessions can be held online, and in-person sessions are available depending on location. We can talk through what works best in the introductory conversation.
