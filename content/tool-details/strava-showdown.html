Students play the data analyst for a mountain bike team. They’re dealt a field of riders, each with a Strava-style ride card — distance, moving time, elevation gain, and four split times — and a race brief saying what the team needs: the fastest rider, the best climber, the steadiest pacer, or the strongest single effort.

  • Call it, then prove it. Students predict the podium before doing any math, then work out each rider’s average speed, climb rate, or split range on an analyst sheet to see whether their gut was right.
  • Math matched to the class. Pick Algebra I, Geometry, Algebra II, Precalculus, Statistics, or Calculus for an extra problem about the same riders — from catch-up problems with d = rt to z-scores and the Mean Value Theorem. A wrong answer gets a hint before the solution is shown.
  • Graph it. Students plot the whole field on a distance–time graph and see that slope is speed — including two riders with identical totals whose rides tell very different stories.
  • Make the case. A coach’s question asks them to pick a rider and defend it with numbers and the graph. I score the answer with a rubric, and each round earns a score and a medal as students rank up from Junior Analyst to Head of Data.
