Students pick a tight, middle or wide line, set their speed, bank the berm, and change the rider's mass and an illustrative grip setting, then press Start turn. A top-down view shows the corner and the inward acceleration; a cross-section shows the berm's normal force, friction and gravity, and how much grip the turn still needs once the bank has done its share.

  • The berm sweet spot. Tilt the berm until the friction arrow disappears: that's the matched angle, tan β = v²/(rg), and it's the same as the rider's lean.
  • Graphs and a line comparison. Acceleration vs. speed, grip vs. berm angle, grip vs. radius, force vs. mass, and a predict-first comparison of all three lines.
  • Six challenges. From picking a line to designing a bike-park berm under a grip limit. Each starts with a prediction; points reward the reasoning.
  • Honest about the model. A simplified steady-state banked turn. The grip numbers are illustrative, not tire data, and nothing in it is riding or safety advice.
