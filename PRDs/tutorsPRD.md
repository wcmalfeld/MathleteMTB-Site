# PRD: Mathlete Private Tutoring — Tutor Directory & Profiles

## 1. Overview

### Project
Expand the existing **Private Tutoring** section of Mathlete to introduce a team-based tutoring experience.

### Objective
Transform the current individual virtual tutoring offering into a professional, approachable tutoring team. Visitors should be able to explore Mathlete's tutors, learn about their backgrounds and teaching approaches, identify the subjects they offer, and find the tutor who best fits their needs.

Mathlete should communicate that it is more than a directory of people who teach academic subjects. It is a team of educators, problem-solvers, mentors, and coaches who help students build confidence and develop a deeper understanding of mathematics and science.

### Core principle

**Different students connect with different teachers. Mathlete brings together a team of tutors with different backgrounds, strengths, and interests to help students find the right person to learn with.**

The experience should feel personal, not like a large, impersonal tutoring marketplace.

---

## 2. Goals

1. Introduce multiple tutors as part of the Mathlete team.
2. Give each tutor a distinct, personalized profile.
3. Help parents and students understand each tutor's experience, personality, teaching style, and areas of expertise.
4. Make it easy to find a tutor based on subject, academic level, or interests.
5. Create a scalable system for adding, editing, and removing tutors without changing the website code.
6. Make it easy for visitors to inquire about or schedule tutoring.
7. Reinforce Mathlete's mission of interest-driven, individualized learning.

### Non-goals

- Building a tutor marketplace where tutors independently register and manage their accounts.
- Building a complicated scheduling or payment system from scratch.
- Allowing students to directly message tutors publicly.
- Creating a general-purpose learning management system as part of this feature.

## 3. Proposed Page Structure

The existing Private Tutoring section should become a dedicated landing page with the following sections:

### Section A: Hero

**Headline:**
Learn from people who make learning click.

**Supporting copy:**
Math and science don't have to feel like a struggle. At Mathlete, our team of tutors takes an individualized, interest-driven approach to learning. We connect academic concepts to the things students already love, helping them build confidence, develop problem-solving skills, and discover what they're capable of.

**Primary CTA:** Find Your Tutor

**Secondary CTA:** How Mathlete Tutoring Works

Visual direction:
- Keep the existing Mathlete visual identity.
- Use authentic photos of the tutors rather than generic stock photography.
- Consider a collage of tutors, students, and activities that reflect the Mathlete approach.
- Keep the hero clean and approachable.

### Section B: Meet the Tutors

**Headline:**
Meet the Mathlete Team

**Supporting copy:**
Great tutoring starts with a great connection. Get to know the people behind Mathlete and find someone whose experience, personality, and interests align with yours.

Display tutors in a responsive card grid.

Each tutor card should include:

- Profile photo.
- Full name.
- Professional title or specialty.
- Short introductory description.

- Academic levels supported.
- A few personal interests or areas of expertise.

A computer science graduate, educator, and lifelong mountain biker who enjoys making challenging academic concepts easier to understand by connecting them to the real world.

### Section C: Individual Tutor Profiles

Each tutor should have an individual profile page or expandable profile view.

The goal is to give visitors a much better understanding of who they would actually be working with.

Each profile should follow a consistent structure while allowing the content and personality of each tutor to shine.

#### Required profile fields

**1. Profile photo**
- Professional but approachable.
- Prefer an outdoor, classroom, or activity-based photo where appropriate.
- High-quality portrait or landscape image with consistent presentation.

**2. Name and title**
Example:
Will Malfeld
Founder & Mathematics and Physics Tutor

**3. Introduction**
A short, engaging introduction that communicates who the tutor is and what they bring to Mathlete.

**4. About the tutor**
A longer biography describing:
- Educational background.
- Teaching and tutoring experience.
- Relevant professional experience.
- Personal interests.
- Why they enjoy teaching.
- What students can expect when working with them.

**5. Subjects and academic levels**

Display subjects as clearly organized tags or categories.

Possible subjects:
- Algebra 1
- Algebra 2
- Geometry
- Precalculus
- Calculus
- Physics
- General Mathematics
- SAT Math

Only display subjects and levels that the individual tutor is qualified and available to teach.

**6. Teaching philosophy**

A short section describing how the tutor approaches learning and what makes their instruction unique.

**7. Areas of specialty**

Examples:
- Interest-driven learning.
- Students who struggle with traditional classroom environments.
- Real-world applications of mathematics and science.
- Building foundational understanding.
- Problem-solving strategies.
- Test preparation.

These should be selected and written individually for each tutor.

**8. Personal interests**

This is an important part of the Mathlete identity.

Include hobbies, sports, activities, and interests that help students connect with their tutor.

Examples:
Mountain biking, skiing, climbing, surfing, engineering, music, technology, and the outdoors.

**9. Availability and tutoring format**
- Online or in-person, where offered.
- Available days or general scheduling information.
- Academic levels supported.
- Any relevant location or time-zone information.

Do not display personal contact information or private scheduling details.

**10. Call to action**

Example:

Ready to get started?

Let's find a learning approach that works for you.

[Inquire About Tutoring]

The inquiry should automatically identify the tutor whose profile the visitor came from.

## 6. Tutor Discovery and Filtering

As the tutor directory grows, students and parents should be able to find tutors based on their needs.

### Initial filtering options

**Filter by subject:**
- Algebra
- Geometry
- Precalculus
- Calculus
- Physics
- SAT Math
- Other

**Filter by academic level:**
- Elementary
- Middle School
- High School
- College

**Filter by tutoring format:**
- Virtual
- In-person

Filters should only display categories with at least one available tutor.

### Matching experience

A visitor should be able to select the subject they need help with and see the tutors who offer that subject.

Do not build an overly complicated matching algorithm in the initial release.

A straightforward filter-based experience is sufficient.

## 7. Tutoring Inquiry Workflow

Every tutor profile should have an inquiry CTA.

When a visitor clicks "Inquire About Tutoring," open the existing inquiry or contact workflow, or create a simple inquiry form if one does not currently exist.

### Inquiry fields

- Parent or student name.
- Email address.
- Student's grade or academic level.
- Subject they need help with.
- Preferred tutor (automatically populated when coming from a tutor profile).
- Brief description of what they need help with.
- Preferred tutoring format.
- Additional information (optional).

### Submission behavior

- Validate required fields.
- Submit inquiries through the existing site's contact infrastructure if available.
- Include the selected tutor in the submission.
- Display a confirmation message.
- Provide an appropriate next step for arranging an introductory conversation or tutoring session.

Suggested confirmation:

"Thanks for reaching out! The Mathlete team will be in touch soon to learn a little more about what you're looking for and help find the right tutoring fit."

Do not imply that a session has been booked unless the site actually supports confirmed booking.

## 8. Tutor Management / CMS

The tutor directory must be data-driven.

Do not hard-code individual tutor cards or profile pages into the frontend.

Create a reusable tutor data model that allows Mathlete administrators to manage the entire team.

### Suggested tutor data model

```ts
type Tutor = {
  id: string;
  slug: string;
  name: string;
  title: string;
  profileImage: string;
  shortBio: string;
  biography: string;
  teachingPhilosophy: string;
  subjects: string[];
  academicLevels: string[];
  specialties: string[];
  interests: string[];
  formats: ("virtual" | "in-person")[];
  location?: string;
  availability?: string;
  featured: boolean;
  published: boolean;
  displayOrder: number;
};
```

### Admin functionality

Administrators should be able to:

- Add a tutor.
- Edit a tutor's information.
- Upload or change a profile photo.
- Add or remove subjects.
- Add academic levels.
- Update specialties and interests.
- Change availability.
- Feature a tutor.
- Publish or unpublish a profile.
- Reorder tutor cards.

### Important implementation detail

Tutor profiles should be generated from the same underlying tutor data.

Updating a tutor in the admin system should automatically update:
- Their card in the directory.
- Their individual profile page.
- Their available subjects in the filtering system.
- Their associated inquiry form.

Avoid maintaining duplicate information in multiple places.

## 9. Design and UX Requirements

### Overall design direction

The experience should feel:
- Educational, but not institutional.
- Professional, but not corporate.
- Approachable, personal, and authentic.
- Modern, with a strong connection to sports, creativity, and real-world learning.

Maintain the existing Mathlete branding and design language.

### Tutor cards

- Use consistent image dimensions.
- Keep card heights consistent within a row where practical.
- Avoid excessive text on the cards.
- Show a maximum of 2–3 introductory sentences.
- Use subject tags for quick scanning.
- Make the entire card or a clear CTA accessible.

### Mobile experience

- Responsive card grid.
- One card per row on small screens.
- Easy-to-use filters.
- Profile pages with stacked sections.
- Prominent inquiry button.

### Accessibility

- Descriptive image alt text.
- Keyboard-accessible interactive elements.
- Sufficient text contrast.
- Proper heading hierarchy.
- Clearly labeled form fields.

## 10. Technical Considerations

Before implementation, inspect the existing Mathlete codebase and identify:

- Current Private Tutoring page structure.
- Existing routing conventions.
- Existing contact and inquiry functionality.
- Existing database and authentication setup.
- Existing content management capabilities.
- Existing design components and styling conventions.

Extend existing architecture rather than introducing unnecessary new dependencies.

If the existing site already has an admin interface or database, reuse it.

If there is no content management system, implement the simplest appropriate solution using the existing stack. Avoid creating a new, complex admin application just for managing tutor profiles.

### Suggested routes

```text
/private-tutoring
/private-tutoring/tutors
/private-tutoring/tutors/[slug]
```

The implementation may adapt these routes to match existing Mathlete conventions.

### SEO

Each tutor profile should have:
- A unique page title.
- A unique meta description.
- A readable URL slug.
- Appropriate social sharing metadata.
- Descriptive image alt text.

Example:

Title: Will Malfeld | Mathematics & Physics Tutor | Mathlete

Description: Meet Will Malfeld, a Mathlete mathematics and physics tutor who connects academic concepts to real-world interests and experiences.

## 11. Acceptance Criteria

The feature is complete when:

1. The Private Tutoring section introduces Mathlete as a team rather than an individual tutoring service.
2. A responsive tutor directory displays all published tutors.
3. Every tutor has an individual profile.
4. Each profile supports a photo, biography, teaching philosophy, subjects, academic levels, specialties, interests, and tutoring format.
5. Tutor information is driven by structured data rather than duplicated hard-coded content.
6. Administrators can add, edit, publish, unpublish, feature, and reorder tutors.
7. Visitors can filter tutors by subject and academic level.
8. Visitors can submit an inquiry associated with a specific tutor.
9. The selected tutor is automatically carried into the inquiry form.
10. The experience works across desktop, tablet, and mobile.
11. Existing Mathlete functionality and the rest of the Private Tutoring section remain intact.
12. No fictional tutor credentials, experience, testimonials, or availability appear on the live site.

## 13. Product Vision

The long-term vision is for Mathlete to become a network of educators who share a common belief: students learn best when education connects to their interests, experiences, and individual ways of thinking.

Each tutor brings something different to the team.

One might be especially good at helping students understand physics through sports. Another might specialize in making foundational mathematics click for students who have struggled in traditional classrooms. Another might bring an engineering background and a passion for problem-solving.

The common thread isn't that every tutor teaches the same way. It's that every tutor is committed to helping students understand, explore, and develop confidence in their abilities.

**Mathlete isn't just about finding someone to help with homework. It's about finding someone who helps students become better thinkers.**
