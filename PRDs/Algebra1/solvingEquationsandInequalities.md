# PART I — PRODUCT VISION

## 1. The concept

### The next chapter of The Dream Line Project

In Unit 1, students designed their dream mountain bike park.

They selected features, created a blueprint, organized resources and calculated the materials needed to bring their park to life.

Now the project is getting serious.

The student becomes the lead engineer, mechanic and park manager responsible for bringing the dream park to life.

Their new responsibilities include:

- Dialing in mountain bike suspension.
- Solving unknown equipment measurements.
- Calculating construction requirements.
- Comparing different park designs and estimating their costs.
- Working with real MTB engineering formulas.
- Calculating trail grades.
- Managing project budgets and construction constraints.

Throughout the experience, algebra is the tool students use to solve practical problems.

The central storyline is:

**You have designed your dream park. Now you need to engineer it, optimize it and get it ready to open.**

## 2. Educational objectives

The lab must directly teach all five Algebra 1 Unit 2 topics.

### One-Step Equations
- Solve equations using inverse operations.
- Check solutions using substitution.
- Write and solve one-step equations from MTB situations.

### Multi-Step Equations
- Solve equations involving two or more operations.
- Apply the distributive property.
- Combine like terms.
- Write and solve multi-step equations from real-world situations.

### Variables on Both Sides
- Collect variable terms on one side.
- Solve equations with variables on both sides.
- Recognize equations with no solution or infinitely many solutions.
- Understand when two relationships are equal.

### Literal Equations
- Rearrange formulas to isolate a specified variable.
- Rearrange real-world MTB formulas.
- Substitute values into rearranged formulas.
- Interpret the result using the correct units.

### Inequalities
- Solve one-step and multi-step inequalities.
- Understand why an inequality sign reverses when multiplying or dividing by a negative number.
- Graph inequalities on a number line.
- Solve compound inequalities.
- Write inequalities to represent real-world limits.

## 3. Experience philosophy

This lab must not feel like five unrelated math worksheets.

Create one connected mountain bike engineering experience with five stages.

Every mathematical concept should have a clear purpose within the student's mission.

The student should:
1. Encounter a realistic MTB problem.
2. Understand what information is known and unknown.
3. Build or identify the correct equation or inequality.
4. Solve it using the appropriate mathematical process.
5. Check the answer.
6. See how the answer affects the MTB project.

The experience should reward mathematical understanding, not merely speed or guessing.

## 4. Design references and MTB environments

Use authentic mountain biking locations and disciplines throughout the lab.

Important visual references include:

- Red Bull Rampage — Virgin, Utah: custom freeride lines and large-scale terrain.
- Crankworx Whistler Rhapsody — The Boneyard: purpose-built slopestyle course.
- Darkfest — Hellsend Dirt Compound: huge dirt jumps and freeride features.
- Whistler Bike Park — A-Line and the progression network.
- Professional suspension workshops and bike mechanics.
- Mountain bike trail builders surveying and measuring terrain.

These environments should provide authentic imagery and inspiration.

Do not turn the lab into a racing game. The focus is engineering, bike setup, design, calculations and park management.

# PART III — STAGE IMPLEMENTATION

# STAGE 01: THE SETUP GARAGE
## One-Step Equations

### 8. Mission

The student becomes the team's lead bike mechanic.

Several riders are preparing their bikes for a major freeride session, but the workshop's setup sheets have missing values.

They must use inverse operations to find the missing measurements and verify their calculations.

### Activity 1 — The Missing Measurement Board

Create a large interactive mechanic's workbench.

Display four different MTB problems on separate cards.

**Problem A: Adding**

A parts inventory has an unknown starting quantity. Eight additional components arrive, bringing the total to 23.

Equation:

`x + 8 = 23`

Answer:

`x = 15`

**Problem B: Subtracting**

A supply inventory has an unknown starting quantity. Six components are used, leaving 18.

Equation:

`x - 6 = 18`

Answer:

`x = 24`

**Problem C: Multiplying**

Each tabletop requires four fictional planning units. The crew has allocated 28 total units.

Equation:

`4x = 28`

Answer:

`x = 7`

**Problem D: Dividing**

An equipment inventory is divided equally across three park zones, with eight units assigned to each zone.

Equation:

`x / 3 = 8`

Answer:

`x = 24`

These are fictional classroom scenarios, not actual park-construction requirements.

### Interaction: Equation Balance

Make this the primary learning interaction in Stage 01.

Display a visual balance scale.

The student sees an equation on both sides of the scale.

Example:

`4x = 28`

The student must select the mathematical operation required to isolate x.

They choose division and apply it to both sides.

Animate:

`4x / 4 = 28 / 4`

Then simplify:

`x = 7`

The scale should remain balanced throughout the process.

Teach the central rule:

**Whatever operation you perform on one side of an equation, you must perform on the other side as well.**

Include:
- Addition and subtraction.
- Multiplication and division.
- Negative coefficients.
- Decimal and fractional answers where appropriate.

The student should be able to apply the inverse operation through buttons or draggable mathematical blocks.

### Activity 2 — Check the Mechanic's Work

Introduce substitution.

A mechanic claims that the solution to:

`3t = 42`

is:

`t = 14`

Ask the student to verify the result.

They substitute:

`3(14) = 42`

The equation is true.

Then show an incorrect answer:

`t = 12`

The student evaluates:

`3(12) = 36`

The equation does not balance.

Ask:

"Does the mechanic's answer satisfy the original equation?"

Teach that checking a solution requires substituting the value into the original equation.

### Activity 3 — Write the Mechanic's Equation

Present written scenarios that the student must translate into equations.

Examples:

1. Three equal practice laps take 42 minutes in total. Find the time for each lap.

   `3t = 42`

   `t = 14 minutes`

2. Six bikes require 36 identical equipment components. How many components are needed per bike?

   `6x = 36`

   `x = 6`

3. Seven equipment kits have already been used, leaving 18. How many kits were there originally?

   `x - 7 = 18`

   `x = 25`

4. A mechanic has 18 units remaining after dividing an unknown inventory equally into three groups.

   `x / 3 = 18`

   `x = 54`

### Activity 4 — Suspension Setup Challenge

Introduce an authentic MTB engineering calculation.

Teach suspension sag as a percentage of total suspension stroke.

Formula:

`P = 100S / T`

Where:
- P = sag percentage.
- S = measured suspension compression.
- T = total shock stroke.

Scenario:

A shock has a 50 mm stroke. A fictional setup sheet specifies 30% sag.

Solve for measured compression using the relationship:

`S = PT / 100`

Substitute:

`S = 30(50) / 100`

Answer:

`S = 15 mm`

For the equation-solving component, give the student a simplified version:

`S / 50 = 0.30`

Then solve by multiplying both sides by 50.

**Important:** Sag targets vary by suspension component, manufacturer, bike and riding setup. Use clearly labelled classroom values, never present them as universal setup instructions. Refer students to their individual bike and suspension manufacturer's guidance for actual setup.

### Stage completion

Award the:

`BIKE MECHANIC` badge.

Completion requires students to:
- Correctly solve inverse-operation problems.
- Verify a solution by substitution.
- Write and solve an equation from a written scenario.

# STAGE 02: THE BUILD CREW
## Multi-Step Equations

### 9. Mission

The park is ready for construction.

The student becomes the lead construction engineer responsible for estimating materials, organizing feature packages and calculating the project's requirements.

Use visual inspiration from Darkfest and the Crankworx Whistler Boneyard.

### Activity 1 — Construction Budget

Present a park-day scenario.

The student has a budget of $105.

A fictional equipment rental package costs $45 initially, plus $12 for every rental hour.

Write:

`45 + 12h = 105`

Solve step by step:

`12h = 60`

`h = 5`

The student can afford five rental hours.

Create a visual budget tracker showing the starting budget, fixed fee, variable costs and remaining balance.

This introduces multi-step equations through a familiar situation.

### Activity 2 — The Feature Materials Calculator

This should be the central activity of Stage 02.

Present a construction problem.

Three jump zones each require a package containing x additional standard marker units plus four fixed setup markers.

Two service zones each require x additional standard marker units plus one fixed setup marker.

The total inventory is 29 units.

Construct:

`3(x + 4) + 2(x + 1) = 29`

Students must:
1. Distribute.
2. Combine like terms.
3. Isolate the variable.
4. Solve.
5. Verify the answer.

Worked solution:

`3x + 12 + 2x + 2 = 29`

`5x + 14 = 29`

`5x = 15`

`x = 3`

Verification:

`3(3 + 4) + 2(3 + 1) = 29`

`21 + 8 = 29`

Correct answer: 3.

Animate the two types of packages being distributed across the different zones.

Make the distribution visually explicit, showing how multiplication applies to every term inside parentheses.

### Activity 3 — Distributive Property Workshop

Show students:

`4(x + 3) = 4x + 12`

Present four construction packages, each containing x standard pieces and three fixed setup pieces.

The student distributes the four packages across both quantities.

Support:
- Visual distribution.
- Drag-and-drop terms.
- Missing-term completion.
- Step-by-step expression simplification.

Then introduce:

`4(x + 3) + 2x = 42`

Solution:

`4x + 12 + 2x = 42`

`6x + 12 = 42`

`6x = 30`

`x = 5`

This should be a guided-to-independent progression.

### Activity 4 — Construction Planning Challenges

Build a challenge bank with new scenarios.

| Scenario | Equation | Answer |
|---|---|---:|
| $30 site preparation plus $15 per construction kit, with $150 available. | `30 + 15x = 150` | 8 |
| Three packages each containing x + 2 items, plus four extra items, total 28. | `3(x + 2) + 4 = 28` | 6 |
| Four feature zones each use x + 3 units, plus 2x for the central area, total 38. | `4(x + 3) + 2x = 38` | 3 |

Require students to create the equation from a description in at least some challenges, rather than always supplying it.

### Stage completion

Award the:

`BUILD ENGINEER` badge.

# STAGE 03: THE DESIGN SHOWDOWN
## Variables on Both Sides

### 10. Mission

Two design teams have submitted proposals for the student's slopestyle park.

Each team has a different initial construction estimate and a different cost per feature.

The student must determine when the two proposals produce the same total cost.

This stage is based on the idea of comparing two relationships and finding their point of equality.

### Activity 1 — Compare Two Park Designs

Design A:
- Initial construction estimate: 18 planning credits.
- Additional cost: 6 credits per feature.

`A = 18 + 6f`

Design B:
- Initial construction estimate: 30 planning credits.
- Additional cost: 4 credits per feature.

`B = 30 + 4f`

Ask:

"How many features must the park contain for both plans to have the same total cost?"

Set equal:

`18 + 6f = 30 + 4f`

Worked solution:

`18 + 2f = 30`

`2f = 12`

`f = 6`

Verify:

Design A:

`18 + 6(6) = 54`

Design B:

`30 + 4(6) = 54`

Both designs have an estimated cost of 54 planning credits.

### Interactive Design Showdown

This is one of the most important visual experiences in the lab.

Build a live comparison tool.

Show two design cards:
- Design A.
- Design B.

Include an interactive feature-count slider.

When the student changes the number of features:
- Both calculated totals update.
- A comparison chart updates.
- The two lines move relative to one another.
- The point of intersection appears when both totals are equal.

The graph should include:
- Horizontal axis: number of features.
- Vertical axis: estimated planning cost.
- Two clearly distinguishable lines.
- A highlighted intersection.
- Live displayed values.

Ask the student to predict the crossover before revealing it.

After the visual prediction, require them to solve the equation algebraically.

Use positive integer feature counts in the primary park-design context, even though the graph may display continuous relationships.

### Activity 2 — No Solution

Introduce two proposals:

`12 + 5f`

`20 + 5f`

Set equal:

`12 + 5f = 20 + 5f`

Subtract `5f`:

`12 = 20`

This is impossible.

Explain:
- Both designs increase at the same rate.
- One always starts higher.
- The lines are parallel.
- There is no number of features where the totals are equal.

Visualize two parallel lines.

### Activity 3 — Infinitely Many Solutions

Introduce two equivalent ways of expressing the same construction plan.

`3(f + 4)`

`3f + 12`

Set equal:

`3(f + 4) = 3f + 12`

Distribute:

`3f + 12 = 3f + 12`

Subtract `3f`:

`12 = 12`

This is always true.

Explain:
- The expressions represent exactly the same relationship.
- Every allowed value of f makes them equal.
- The graph lines overlap completely.
- There are infinitely many solutions.

### Activity 4 — Design Comparison Challenge

Present several competing scenarios:
- Two construction-cost proposals.
- Two fictional park membership pricing plans.
- Two equipment rental plans.
- Two feature-production estimates.
- Two service packages with different fixed and variable costs.

Students must:
1. Create an equation by setting the expressions equal.
2. Solve for the unknown.
3. Determine whether the solution is realistic in the scenario.
4. Classify the equation as one solution, no solution or infinitely many solutions.

Include a short explanation after each answer.

### Stage completion

Award the:

`DESIGN ANALYST` badge.

# STAGE 04: THE FORMULA GARAGE
## Literal Equations

### 11. Mission

The student has become the park's technical engineer.

Instead of working with one unknown quantity, they now need to rearrange formulas to find different unknowns.

This is the stage where the lab should demonstrate authentic mountain bike engineering mathematics.

Build a Formula Garage where students can select a formula, isolate a variable and then use the rearranged version to solve an actual scenario.

### Activity 1 — Speed, Distance and Time

Introduce:

`d = rt`

Where:
- d = distance.
- r = average speed.
- t = time.

Scenario:

A hypothetical downhill trail section is 4.8 km long. A rider's average speed for the section is 16 km/h.

Find the time.

Rearrange:

`t = d / r`

Substitute:

`t = 4.8 / 16`

`t = 0.3 hours`

`0.3 × 60 = 18 minutes`

Create an interactive trail-map visualization showing the relationship between distance, speed and time.

Students should be able to:
- Solve for distance.
- Solve for rate.
- Solve for time.

Every result should show units.

### Activity 2 — Trail Grade Engineering

Introduce the mathematical relationship between elevation change and horizontal distance.

`G = 100R / L`

Where:
- G = percentage grade.
- R = vertical rise.
- L = horizontal run.

Make an explicit distinction between horizontal run and the actual distance travelled along the inclined trail.

Introduce a trail-profile visualization that shows:
- Starting elevation.
- Ending elevation.
- Vertical rise.
- Horizontal run.
- Percentage grade.

**Challenge A: Calculate grade**

A trail connector has:
- Vertical rise: 18 feet.
- Horizontal run: 300 feet.

`G = 100(18) / 300`

Answer:

`G = 6%`

**Challenge B: Solve for rise**

A design has a 6% grade and a 300-foot horizontal run.

Rearrange:

`R = GL / 100`

`R = 6(300) / 100`

`R = 18 feet`

**Challenge C: Solve for horizontal run**

A trail section has:
- Rise: 18 feet.
- Grade: 6%.

Rearrange:

`L = 100R / G`

`L = 100(18) / 6`

`L = 300 feet`

Require students to choose the variable they need before rearranging the formula.

The visualization should update based on the selected unknown.

### Activity 3 — Suspension Engineering

Introduce the suspension sag relationship:

`P = 100S / T`

Where:
- P = sag percentage.
- S = measured suspension compression.
- T = total shock stroke.

Use a fictional setup sheet with:
- Target sag: 30%.
- Shock stroke: 50 mm.

Solve for suspension compression:

`S = PT / 100`

`S = 30(50) / 100`

`S = 15 mm`

Now reverse the challenge.

Given:
- Measured sag: 15 mm.
- Total stroke: 50 mm.

Calculate sag percentage:

`P = 100(15) / 50`

`P = 30%`

Also require students to rearrange for total shock stroke:

`T = 100S / P`

This activity must include an authentic visual of a rear shock with an O-ring, showing how suspension compression is measured.

Clearly distinguish:
- Shock stroke.
- Suspension compression.
- Sag percentage.

Do not suggest that every rider should use 30% sag. The target depends on the bike, suspension system, rider and manufacturer guidance.

### Activity 4 — Formula Rearrangement Studio

Build a dedicated Formula Garage interaction.

Students select:
1. A formula.
2. The variable they want to isolate.
3. The mathematical operation to perform.
4. The resulting rearranged formula.

Support the following formulas:

**Speed, distance and time**

`d = rt`

- `d = rt`
- `r = d / t`
- `t = d / r`

**Trail grade**

`G = 100R / L`

- `G = 100R / L`
- `R = GL / 100`
- `L = 100R / G`

**Suspension sag**

`P = 100S / T`

- `P = 100S / T`
- `S = PT / 100`
- `T = 100S / P`

All formula rearrangements must be mathematically equivalent, correctly formatted and independently tested.

The student should be able to interact with the formula by moving variables and mathematical operations, but never lose the ability to see a traditional step-by-step solution.

### Activity 5 — Engineering Formula Assessment

Present new situations with different numbers.

Ask students to:
- Select the formula.
- Choose the unknown.
- Rearrange the formula.
- Substitute known values.
- Calculate the answer.
- Explain what the result means.

### Stage completion

Award the:

`MTB ENGINEER` badge.

# STAGE 05: PARK OPERATIONS
## Inequalities

### 12. Mission

The park is almost ready to open.

The student is now the park manager, responsible for keeping the project within its available budget, resource limits and design constraints.

The big idea:

**Equations describe quantities that are equal. Inequalities describe limits and ranges.**

### Activity 1 — The Construction Budget

The project has a maximum budget of 105 planning credits.

Initial setup costs 45 credits. Every additional feature package costs 12 credits.

Write:

`45 + 12h ≤ 105`

Subtract 45:

`12h ≤ 60`

Divide by 12:

`h ≤ 5`

Interpretation:

The park can purchase no more than five additional feature packages.

If h represents a count of packages, it must be a nonnegative whole number.

Visually show the budget decreasing as packages are added.

When the student goes over budget, explain why the plan is no longer valid.

### Activity 2 — The Negative Number Challenge

Present a more advanced scenario.

The park begins with 180 planning credits.

Each additional feature kit costs 12 credits.

The park must retain at least 60 credits.

Write:

`180 - 12x ≥ 60`

Subtract 180:

`-12x ≥ -120`

Divide both sides by -12.

**The inequality sign must reverse.**

`x ≤ 10`

Because x is a number of kits:

`0 ≤ x ≤ 10`

The student may purchase up to ten kits.

Make the sign reversal an interactive discovery.

Ask:

"What happens to the inequality when we divide by a negative number?"

Present two possible directions and require a prediction.

Animate the sign changing direction when the student divides both sides by -12.

Use a number-line demonstration to explain why the reversal is necessary.

### Activity 3 — The Number Line Studio

Build a dedicated number-line visualization.

Support:
- Open circles.
- Closed circles.
- Left-facing arrows.
- Right-facing arrows.
- Finite intervals.
- Union of two intervals.

Examples:

`x + 4 ≤ 12`

Answer:

`x ≤ 8`

And:

`3x + 6 > 18`

Answer:

`x > 4`

Students must solve the inequality and then graph the answer.

Allow the student to change between algebraic and graphical representations.

### Activity 4 — Compound Inequalities

Introduce a fictional construction requirement.

The park's approved planning allocation must be between 36 and 84 credits, inclusive.

Each feature package requires 8 credits, plus 4 fixed credits.

Write:

`36 ≤ 8x + 4 ≤ 84`

Subtract 4 from all three parts:

`32 ≤ 8x ≤ 80`

Divide all three parts by 8:

`4 ≤ x ≤ 10`

Interpretation:

The project can contain between four and ten feature packages, inclusive.

Because these are package counts, the permitted values are integers.

Visualize the solution as a bounded interval with closed endpoints.

Also introduce an OR-type compound inequality:

`x < 2 OR x > 8`

Show the two separate regions on the number line.

Clearly distinguish:
- AND inequalities, which require both conditions to hold.
- OR inequalities, which require at least one condition to hold.

### Activity 5 — Trail Grade Constraints

Bring back the trail-grade formula.

A fictional park design brief specifies:
- Horizontal run: 300 feet.
- Maximum permitted average grade for this classroom scenario: 8%.

The student must find the maximum vertical rise.

Starting relationship:

`G = 100R / L`

Substitute the known values:

`100R / 300 ≤ 8`

Simplify:

`R / 3 ≤ 8`

`R ≤ 24`

The vertical rise must be no greater than 24 feet to meet this particular design brief.

Show the trail profile adjusting as the vertical rise changes.

**Important:** The 8% threshold is a fictional project requirement, not a universal MTB trail construction standard. Real trail design involves professional assessment and site-specific guidance.

### Activity 6 — Final Park Approval

Display a project approval dashboard.

The park must satisfy these fictional conditions:

- Project spending cannot exceed 180 credits.
- At least four feature packages must be purchased.
- At least 60 credits must remain.
- No more than ten feature kits can be purchased.
- The mapped connector must satisfy its specified scenario grade limit.

Give the student several candidate plans.

They must use inequalities to decide which plans satisfy all the constraints.

Require a short explanation for at least one decision.

### Stage completion

Award the:

`PARK MANAGER` badge.

# PART V — SHARED LEARNING SYSTEMS

## 15. Math Workbench

Integrate the established Mathlete MTB Math Workbench.

It should remain accessible alongside the active challenge.

### Simple mode

Display:
- Concept definition.
- Plain-language explanation.
- Relevant equation or formula.
- Explanation of variables.
- Step-by-step example.
- Real-world MTB connection.

### Advanced mode

Display:
- Complete algebraic expression.
- Current equation state.
- Mathematical operation performed.
- Result after each step.
- Explanation of inverse operations.
- Substitution check.
- Common mistakes.

The workbench must dynamically update depending on the active problem.

## 16. Prediction-first learning

Use prediction-first interactions throughout the experience when they genuinely help learning.

Examples:
- Which operation should be reversed?
- Will the value increase or decrease?
- Where will two design proposals become equal?
- Will the inequality sign flip?
- Which design satisfies the budget?

The student commits to a prediction before the simulation reveals the result.

Do not require predictions for every question.

## 17. Hint system

Create progressive hints for every challenge.

Example:

Problem:

`4x + 12 = 42`

Hint 1:

"Your goal is to get x by itself."

Hint 2:

"What operation is being added to 4x?"

Hint 3:

"Can you subtract 12 from both sides?"

Reveal:

`4x = 30`

Continue with the division step.

For inequalities:

Include a specific hint explaining why multiplying or dividing by a negative value reverses the inequality sign.

Hints should explain the reasoning rather than reveal the answer immediately.

## 18. Feedback

Every answer submission must trigger meaningful feedback.

Correct:

"Exactly. You subtracted 12 from both sides, preserving the balance of the equation."

Incorrect:

"Not quite. Remember that you need to perform the same operation on both sides."

For a wrong inequality sign:

"Careful! When you divide an inequality by a negative number, the direction of the inequality must reverse."

Allow students to try again.

Do not lock students out after incorrect responses.

## 19. Difficulty progression

Each stage must contain three levels.

**Beginner**
- Guided examples.
- Whole numbers.
- Simple equations.
- Multiple-choice support.
- Worked demonstrations.

**Intermediate**
- Multi-step calculations.
- Negative numbers.
- Decimals and fractions.
- Independent algebraic manipulation.
- Real-world application.

**Advanced**
- More complex equations.
- Multi-variable relationships.
- No-solution and infinite-solution cases.

# PART VIII — MATHEMATICAL QUALITY ASSURANCE

## 30. Automated calculation tests

All examples must have automated tests.

### One-step equations

| Equation | Expected result |
|---|---:|
| `x + 8 = 23` | 15 |
| `x - 6 = 18` | 24 |
| `4x = 28` | 7 |
| `x / 3 = 8` | 24 |
| `3t = 42` | 14 |
| `-3x = 18` | -6 |

### Multi-step equations

| Equation | Expected result |
|---|---:|
| `45 + 12h = 105` | 5 |
| `3(x + 4) + 2(x + 1) = 29` | 3 |
| `4(x + 3) + 2x = 42` | 5 |
| `30 + 15x = 150` | 8 |
| `3(x + 2) + 4 = 28` | 6 |
| `4(x + 3) + 2x = 38` | 3 |

### Variables on both sides

| Equation | Expected result |
|---|---|
| `18 + 6f = 30 + 4f` | f = 6 |
| `12 + 5f = 20 + 5f` | No solution |
| `3(f + 4) = 3f + 12` | Infinitely many solutions |

Verify that the graphical representation correctly shows intersecting, parallel and coincident lines.

### Literal equations

| Formula / input | Expected answer |
|---|---:|
| `d = 4.8`, `r = 16`, solve t | 0.3 hours / 18 minutes |
| `d = 9`, `t = 0.5`, solve r | 18 km/h |
| `R = 18`, `L = 300`, solve G | 6% |
| `G = 6`, `L = 300`, solve R | 18 feet |
| `G = 6`, `R = 18`, solve L | 300 feet |
| `P = 30`, `T = 50`, solve S | 15 mm |
| `S = 15`, `T = 50`, solve P | 30% |
| `P = 30`, `S = 15`, solve T | 50 mm |

### Inequalities

| Inequality | Expected result |
|---|---|
| `45 + 12h ≤ 105` | h ≤ 5 |
| `180 - 12x ≥ 60` | x ≤ 10 |
| `36 ≤ 8x + 4 ≤ 84` | 4 ≤ x ≤ 10 |
| `x + 4 ≤ 12` | x ≤ 8 |
| `3x + 6 > 18` | x > 4 |
| `100R / 300 ≤ 8` | R ≤ 24 |
| `x < 2 OR x > 8` | Two separate regions |

For whole-number inventory contexts, also verify any nonnegative and integer-domain restrictions.

## 31. Common misconception testing

Explicitly test:

- Adding or subtracting an operation on only one side.
- Dividing before removing an additive constant.
- Incorrect distribution.
- Combining unlike terms.
- Incorrectly moving variables without maintaining equality.
- Confusing no solution with infinitely many solutions.
- Rearranging formulas incorrectly.
- Losing units during substitution.
- Forgetting to reverse an inequality when dividing by a negative number.
- Using an open circle where a closed circle is required.
- Treating an OR compound inequality as an AND interval.
- Confusing horizontal run with the length travelled along a trail.

## 36. Non-goals and safety boundaries

This is an Algebra 1 learning experience, not a professional MTB engineering application.

Do not:
- Present classroom suspension numbers as universal bike setup recommendations.
- Recommend changing actual suspension settings from generic example values.
- Present fictional trail-grade limits as professional trail-design standards.
- Teach students to engineer real jump dimensions, takeoff angles or landing zones.
- Use actual park construction budgets as though the simulated credits are industry pricing.
- Turn the experience into a racing game.
- Add advanced mathematical concepts unnecessarily.
- Sacrifice learning quality for gamification.
- Duplicate existing Mathlete functionality.

Clearly identify instructional examples and fictional constraints.

Link students to manufacturer guidance when discussing real suspension setup, and keep real-world construction guidance outside the educational calculation tool.
