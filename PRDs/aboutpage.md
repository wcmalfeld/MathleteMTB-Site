# PRD: Mathlete About Page
## Brand Philosophy, Educational Approach & Mission

**Product:** Mathlete MTB  
**Feature:** About Page  
**Suggested Route:** `/about`  
**Priority:** High  
**Audience:** Parents, students, educators, tutors, schools, alternative education programs, and potential organizational customers.

---

# 1. Project Overview

Build a dedicated About page for Mathlete that communicates the company's educational philosophy, mission, approach to learning, and long-term vision.

The page should establish Mathlete as an interest-driven academic learning platform that helps students develop a deeper understanding of math and science through activities they already love.

It should introduce the three foundational educational principles:

1. Interest-driven education — start with what makes students curious.
2. Experiential learning — learn by doing, experimenting, and solving problems.
3. Personalized instruction — support students according to their interests, needs, and academic progress.

It should also communicate an important differentiator: Mathlete believes technology and AI should enhance human instruction rather than replace teachers, tutors, and mentors.

The About page must speak to parents who are looking for a different educational experience for their children, particularly parents of students who do not always connect with conventional classroom instruction.

It should also communicate a broader vision for Mathlete beyond mountain biking, positioning MTB as the initial application of a much larger interest-driven learning platform.

## 2. Core Brand Positioning

### Primary positioning statement

"Mathlete is an interest-driven academic learning platform that helps students develop a deeper understanding of math and science through the activities they already love."

### Primary tagline

"Learning that starts with what they're passionate about."

### Supporting brand message

"Built around how they learn. Guided by people who care."

### Educational philosophy

Students benefit when academic concepts are connected to things they already care about, rather than being taught exclusively in isolation.

Mathlete uses familiar interests and real-world experiences as entry points into academic concepts, helping students understand not just what they're learning, but why it matters.

### Brand identity

Mathlete should be positioned as:

- An alternative approach to conventional academic learning.
- An interest-driven educational platform.
- A curriculum-aligned math and science learning environment.
- An experiential learning resource.
- A personalized academic practice platform.
- A technology-supported but human-centered educational experience.
- A place where students develop problem-solving skills and confidence.

Do not position Mathlete exclusively as an ADHD platform, mountain biking game, or online tutoring website.

The overarching brand should accommodate a much broader audience.

The About page must appeal to multiple audiences without feeling overly fragmented.

Parents looking for an engaging alternative or supplement to conventional academic instruction.

Particularly relevant to:

- Children with ADHD or attention-regulation difficulties.
- Students who struggle with traditional classroom environments.
- Students who need more engaging or interactive academic experiences.
- Homeschooling families.
- Students who learn best through hands-on activities and experimentation.

### Secondary audience: Educators and organizations

- Alternative education schools.
- Microschools.

- Outdoor academies.
- Sports-focused schools.
- Tutors and educational therapists.
- Traditional schools seeking supplemental learning resources.

Students should see Mathlete as an exciting environment where their interests are valued and learning feels relevant.

# 4. Page Structure and Content Requirements

Build the About page as a visually engaging, vertically structured experience.

The page should contain the following sections in this order.

## Section 1: Hero

**Purpose:** Immediately communicate Mathlete's identity and make visitors interested in learning more.

Headline:

"Learning that starts with what they're passionate about."

Supporting headline:

"Not every kid learns from a worksheet. Sometimes, you just have to start with something they love."

Body:

"Mathlete connects math and science to the activities, interests, and experiences that make students curious. Through interactive challenges, real-world applications, and personalized learning experiences, we help students develop a deeper understanding of the world around them."

Primary CTA:
"Explore Mathlete"

Secondary CTA:
"Explore Our Learning Approach"

Design requirements:

- Prominent hero headline.
- Strong visual related to mountain biking, outdoor exploration, or hands-on learning.
- Preserve the existing Mathlete visual identity.
- Use an engaging background or image treatment without compromising text readability.
- Keep the message concise and immediately understandable.

The hero should communicate that Mathlete is about more than biking.

## Section 2: The Problem With One-Size-Fits-All Education

**Purpose:** Help parents recognize the educational problem Mathlete is trying to solve.

Headline:

"There's more than one way to learn."

Body:

"Traditional education often introduces academic concepts in isolation, asking students to understand abstract ideas before they've had an opportunity to see why they matter.

But not every student connects with learning in the same way.

Some students learn through textbooks and lectures. Others learn by building, experimenting, exploring, moving, and solving problems in the world around them.

A student who struggles to stay engaged with a traditional math lesson might spend hours learning how to build a mountain bike, master a new trick, create something, or understand how a video game works.

What if we started there?

We believe students shouldn't have to fit into a single approach to education to reach their potential."

Emphasize this statement visually:

"We don't believe students need to be passionate about every subject. We believe passion can be a powerful place to start."

Design requirements:

- Use distinct typography for the emphasized statement.
- Include imagery showing students participating in hands-on activities.
- Use a relatively short paragraph layout with plenty of whitespace.
- Avoid making traditional schools or teachers appear to be the enemy. Mathlete should complement and offer alternatives to conventional instruction, not criticize every traditional educational environment.

## Section 3: Our Three Educational Pillars

**Purpose:** Explain the educational philosophy and the actual methodology behind Mathlete.

This should be one of the most prominent sections of the page.

Headline:

"A different way to approach education."

Supporting text:

"Our approach is built around three principles that help connect students to meaningful academic learning."

Create three visually distinct cards or content blocks.

### Pillar 1: Interest-Driven Education

Title: "Start with what makes them curious."

Description:

"Every student has something they're passionate about. It might be mountain biking, skiing, soccer, skateboarding, gaming, music, art, or building things.

We use those interests as an entry point into academic learning, connecting the familiar to the unfamiliar and helping students discover the mathematics and science behind the things they enjoy."

Key concept: Interest is the starting point.

### Pillar 2: Experiential Learning

Title: "Learn by doing, not just by listening."

Description:

"Instead of relying exclusively on lectures, videos, and worksheets, Mathlete creates opportunities for students to experiment, investigate, build, manipulate, and solve problems.

Students might explore quadratic functions by modeling a mountain bike jump, study geometry by designing a trail, or understand physics by experimenting with suspension and momentum.

We want students to experience concepts, not simply memorize definitions."

Key concept: Experience is the teaching method.

### Pillar 3: Personalized Instruction

Title: "Every student has a different path."

Description:

"Every student has a different starting point, pace, and way of approaching a problem.

Mathlete supports personalized learning experiences that adapt to individual interests, academic needs, and progress.

Students can work through challenges at their own pace, revisit concepts they find difficult, explore ideas in greater depth, and receive guidance that helps them move forward."

Key concept: Personalization is how we support individual learners.

Design requirements:

- Three clearly differentiated cards.
- Each card should have a distinctive icon or supporting image.
- Visually emphasize the pillar title and central concept.
- Use subtle hover interactions where appropriate.
- Cards should stack cleanly on mobile.

## Section 4: For Students Who Don't Always Fit the Traditional Classroom

**Purpose:** Establish Mathlete as an inclusive learning platform for a broader audience, including neurodivergent students and those who benefit from alternative instructional approaches.

Headline:

"For the students who don't always fit the traditional classroom."

Body:

"Not every student connects with conventional education.

Some need to move. Some need to see a concept before they understand it. Some learn best by experimenting, asking questions, and discovering things for themselves. Others need more time, more individual attention, or a reason to care about what they're being asked to learn.

Students with ADHD, short attention spans, different learning preferences, or simply a disconnect with traditional instruction may particularly appreciate having another way to approach academic subjects.

We don't believe those students need to be made to learn like everyone else.

We believe education should make room for different ways of thinking, exploring, and understanding."

Introduce five design principles:

1. Engaging — built around meaningful interests and real-world applications.
2. Interactive — encourages students to participate, experiment, and make discoveries.
3. Manageable — breaks challenging concepts into smaller, achievable learning experiences.
4. Flexible — allows students to learn at a pace and through an approach that works for them.
5. Encouraging — celebrates progress, persistence, curiosity, and problem solving.

Emphasized statement:

"Our goal is to help more students recognize that they're capable learners, even if their strengths don't always show up in a traditional classroom."

Design requirements:

- This section should feel welcoming, supportive, and empathetic.
- Avoid clinical language and negative portrayals of ADHD.
- Avoid implying that Mathlete treats ADHD or improves ADHD symptoms.
- Do not make diagnostic or therapeutic claims.
- Focus on providing an alternative learning environment rather than trying to correct students.
- Use engaging imagery that portrays students as capable, curious, and active.

## Section 5: Technology Should Enhance Human Connection

**Purpose:** Establish the human-in-the-loop learning model as a major Mathlete differentiator.

Headline:

"Technology should enhance human connection, not replace it."

Body:

"As education becomes increasingly digital and AI-powered, we believe it's more important than ever to keep real people involved in the learning process.

Technology can provide interactive experiences, immediate feedback, personalized practice, and insight into where a student might be struggling.

But it can't replace the encouragement, understanding, mentorship, and human connection that make good teaching so valuable.

We believe the future of education should combine the best of technology with the best of human instruction."

Explain the Mathlete learning model:

1. Students practice independently throughout the week.
2. The platform tracks progress, missed problems, and areas of difficulty.
3. Teachers and tutors receive insight into where students need help.
4. Short, purposeful check-ins allow instructors to clarify difficult concepts and provide personal support.
5. Students return to independent practice with a stronger understanding.

Highlight the check-in concept:

"Sometimes a student needs just two minutes of guidance. Other times, they need a 20-minute conversation to work through a difficult concept."

Emphasized statement:

"Technology helps us understand where students are. People help them figure out where to go next."

Design requirements:

- Visually communicate the relationship between students, software, and educators.
- A simple three-part illustration or process diagram would work well:
  Student Practice → Learning Insights → Human Guidance.
- Emphasize human support as an intentional part of the learning model.
- Do not imply that AI or teachers are currently performing functionality that has not yet been implemented. Present planned features as product direction until operational.

## Section 6: Beyond Getting the Right Answer

**Purpose:** Explain the deeper educational outcomes Mathlete wants to promote.

Headline:

"Beyond getting the right answer. Learning how to solve problems."

Body:

"At the heart of Mathlete is something bigger than math and science.

It's the development of problem-solving skills.

We want students to understand how to approach unfamiliar challenges, break complicated problems into smaller pieces, recognize patterns, test ideas, learn from mistakes, and keep working toward a solution.

These are skills that extend far beyond a classroom.

A student learning how to calculate the trajectory of a mountain bike jump is also learning how to model a problem, interpret information, make predictions, and apply mathematical reasoning.

Those same skills can be applied to engineering, business, technology, and the countless challenges they'll encounter throughout life.

We want students to become confident thinkers, curious learners, and capable problem solvers.

Because education shouldn't just prepare students to pass their next test. It should prepare them to understand and navigate the world around them."

Design requirements:

- Strong visual treatment for the problem-solving philosophy.
- Consider highlighting individual skills: curiosity, critical thinking, experimentation, persistence, and application.
- Use illustrations or imagery showing students actively solving problems.

## Section 7: What Learning With Mathlete Looks Like

**Purpose:** Transition from educational philosophy to tangible platform capabilities.

Headline:

"Meaningful learning, built around real academic goals."

Introduce six learning components.

### 1. Curriculum-Aligned Academics

Students explore real academic subjects, including Algebra, Geometry, Precalculus, and Physics, through organized courses and identifiable learning objectives.

### 2. Interactive Learning Labs

Experiment with mathematical and scientific concepts through simulations, challenges, and real-world applications.

### 3. Personalized Practice

Work through challenges at an appropriate level, revisit difficult concepts, and explore topics in greater depth.

### 4. Progress and Mastery Tracking

Identify strengths, understand areas of difficulty, save missed problems, and build a meaningful picture of academic development.

### 5. Human Guidance and Mentorship

Give teachers and tutors the information they need to provide targeted instruction, encouragement, and additional support.

### 6. Learning Beyond the Screen

Connect academic concepts to physical experiences, outdoor exploration, creative projects, and the world around us.

Design requirements:

- Display these as six cards or a visually appealing grid.
- Include icons or small illustrations.
- Keep descriptions concise.
- Make the section easy to scan.
- Do not advertise future functionality as currently available. Use appropriate wording or omit features that have not yet been built.

## Section 8: Where It Starts — Mountain Biking

**Purpose:** Explain the origin and initial specialty of Mathlete while making clear that the broader mission extends beyond MTB.

Headline:

"Where it starts: Mountain biking."

Body:

"Mathlete began with a simple idea: what if we taught students math and physics through something they already loved?

Mountain biking provides an incredible environment for exploring academic concepts.

There's mathematics in every jump, geometry in every trail, physics in every turn, and problem solving in every challenge.

Students can explore quadratic functions through jump trajectories, geometry through trail design, statistics through race performance, and physics through suspension, speed, and momentum.

But mountain biking is just the beginning.

The larger vision is to create an educational platform that can connect academic learning to the many different interests, activities, and passions that make students unique.

Because the best starting point for learning isn't necessarily a textbook.

Sometimes it's a bike, a ball, a computer, a musical instrument, or a question a student has always wanted to answer."

Design requirements:

- This should be one of the most visually engaging sections.
- Use authentic mountain biking imagery.
- Showcase how real MTB activities translate into academic concepts.
- Consider a horizontally arranged group of examples:
  - Jumping → Quadratic Functions and Physics.
  - Trail Building → Geometry and Measurement.
  - Race Performance → Statistics and Data Analysis.
  - Suspension Setup → Forces, Ratios, and Energy.
- End with a visual transition to the broader Mathlete vision.

## Section 9: Our Mission

**Purpose:** Provide a memorable conclusion that summarizes the entire Mathlete philosophy.

Headline:

"Our mission."

Primary mission statement:

"To make meaningful academic learning more accessible, engaging, and relevant by connecting education to the things students are passionate about."

Supporting text:

"We want to help students develop a genuine curiosity for learning, build confidence in their abilities, and discover that they are capable of far more than they might have thought.

We believe there's no single path to understanding, no single way to be a good student, and no reason that education should look exactly the same for everyone."

Final emphasized statement:

"Find what they're passionate about. Start there. Build from it."

Design requirements:

- Large, memorable final statement.
- Strong background visual or design treatment.
- A clear CTA to explore the learning platform.
- The conclusion should feel inspirational without becoming overly corporate or sales-oriented.

# 6. Visual Design Requirements

## Overall Design Philosophy

The About page should balance two characteristics:

1. The existing Mathlete aesthetic, including its old-school terminal-inspired design identity.
2. A welcoming, approachable, human-centered educational brand.

The result should feel like a modern technology platform built around a meaningful educational philosophy.

It should not look like a generic corporate education website.

## Brand Personality

- Curious.
- Adventurous.
- Approachable.
- Encouraging.
- Intelligent.
- Creative.
- Human.
- Slightly unconventional.

## Typography

- Preserve existing Mathlete fonts and typography conventions.
- Use strong visual hierarchy.
- Hero headlines should be prominent.
- Body text should be easy to read.
- Avoid overly dense paragraphs.
- Use varied heading sizes and whitespace to create a natural reading experience.

## Color

- Preserve the existing Mathlete brand colors.
- Reuse existing design tokens.
- Avoid introducing an entirely new color palette.
- Use accent colors to highlight important statements and calls to action.

## Imagery

Use authentic imagery related to:

- Mountain biking.
- Outdoor exploration.
- Students experimenting and building.
- Hands-on academic activities.
- Interactive mathematics and science.
- Students collaborating with teachers and mentors.

Avoid:
- Generic corporate stock photography.
- Excessive images of traditional classrooms.
- Imagery that portrays students as struggling or incapable.
- Images that make the page feel exclusively about mountain biking.

Use existing image assets where appropriate. Introduce new assets only when they materially improve the page.

## Layout

- Generous whitespace.
- Clear separation between sections.
- Alternating image and text layouts where appropriate.
- Visually distinct section introductions.
- Cards for the three educational pillars and platform capabilities.
- Subtle transitions and hover effects.
- Consistent spacing and border radius.

The page should feel intentional and editorial rather than like a long document pasted onto a website.

# 7. Responsive Requirements

The page must work well across:

- Desktop.
- Laptop.
- Tablet.
- Mobile.

Desktop:
- Utilize available screen width.
- Allow multi-column layouts.
- Give the hero strong visual impact.

Tablet:
- Reduce column counts as needed.
- Maintain comfortable reading widths.

Mobile:
- Stack all multi-column content.
- Ensure headings wrap naturally.
- Keep cards readable and touch-friendly.
- Prevent images from becoming excessively tall.
- Maintain comfortable spacing between content blocks.
- Ensure CTAs are easily accessible.

The mobile experience is particularly important because parents may first encounter Mathlete through social media advertisements or shared links.

# 8. Accessibility and Usability

Follow accessible design practices.

Requirements:

- Semantic heading hierarchy.
- Appropriate image alt text.
- Sufficient text contrast.
- Keyboard-accessible interactive elements.
- Visible focus states.
- Descriptive link labels.
- Reduced-motion support for animations.
- No essential information conveyed by color alone.
- Avoid excessive motion, flashing, or distracting animations.

The page should also reflect Mathlete's flexible-learning philosophy through its own design: readable typography, manageable sections, clear navigation, and minimal cognitive clutter.

# 11. Content and Messaging Guidelines

Maintain a consistent voice throughout the page.

Mathlete should sound:

- Passionate about education.
- Empathetic toward students and parents.
- Confident in its educational philosophy.
- Curious and adventurous.
- Encouraging without being overly sentimental.
- Knowledgeable without sounding academic or inaccessible.

Important messaging rules:

1. Never describe students as broken, lazy, incapable, or difficult.
2. Do not imply that traditional education is universally bad.
3. Avoid claiming that Mathlete treats ADHD or improves medical symptoms.
4. Avoid promising better academic outcomes before there is evidence.
5. Do not position AI as a replacement for teachers or tutors.
6. Distinguish between existing platform features and future product aspirations.
7. Emphasize academic rigor alongside engagement.
8. Consistently reinforce the connection between student interests and meaningful academic understanding.

The central message should remain:

"Students don't all learn the same way. Mathlete gives them another way to connect with what they're learning."

# 14. Final Product Vision

The finished About page should leave three impressions:

**For parents:** Mathlete understands that their child may benefit from a different approach to learning, and it offers a thoughtful alternative.

**For educators:** Mathlete has a credible educational philosophy that combines academic curriculum, experiential learning, personalized practice, and human instruction.

**For students:** Their interests matter. They can learn through the things they already love, explore new ideas, and become more confident problem solvers.

Ultimately, this page should establish Mathlete as more than a collection of educational tools.

It should communicate an educational philosophy that informs everything the company builds.

**Learning that starts with what they're passionate about. Built around how they learn. Guided by people who care.**
