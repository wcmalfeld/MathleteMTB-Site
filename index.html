About

Hi, I'm Will — the tutor and coach behind Mathlete MTB. I help young riders become stronger thinkers, students, and athletes by building confidence through mathematics, problem-solving, and learning outside of traditional academic environments.

I believe many students begin to identify themselves as "bad at math" far too early. Over time, that belief can become a barrier not only to academic achievement, but to a student's willingness to take on challenges and make mistakes. With the right approach, however, that mindset can change.

My goal is to help students develop both mathematical ability and confidence in themselves as learners. I use problem-solving, real-world applications, and connections to the things students are already passionate about — particularly mountain biking — to make math more engaging and meaningful. When students are genuinely interested in what they are learning, they are more willing to experiment, make mistakes, and persist through difficult problems. Those experiences are where lasting confidence is built.

My experience as a teacher, mentor, and mountain bike coach has given me the opportunity to work with students who don't always thrive in traditional classroom environments. I've seen students go from believing they simply "aren't good at math" to approaching challenging problems with confidence and a willingness to figure things out.

If your student is struggling to find interest in math, isn't achieving the results they're capable of, or would simply benefit from additional support outside of school, Mathlete MTB offers a different approach — combining individualized math instruction with the motivation, community, and mindset that come from being part of the mountain biking world.

The goal isn't just to get better at math. It's to become more confident taking on difficult things.

My Approach

Every student learns differently. I focus on building strong fundamentals, explaining the "why" behind the math, and helping students feel genuinely capable — not just getting through the next test.

Sessions are relaxed but focused. We move at the pace that actually works for you or your student, revisiting the basics when needed and pushing ahead when things click.

Why Mountain Biking + Math

Mountain biking and math have more in common than people expect. Both reward patience, both involve reading the terrain ahead of you, and both get easier — and more fun — the more comfortable you get with falling down and trying again.

Learning math is a lot like learning to ride. You don't become confident by only doing things you already know how to do. You become confident by trying something difficult, making mistakes, figuring out what went wrong, and trying again.

My job isn't to make sure a student never gets a problem wrong. It's to help them become comfortable enough with being wrong that they're willing to keep trying.
